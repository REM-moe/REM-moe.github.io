I come from a land where fire stands witness, where a mere mortal sheds his skin and becomes a deity. To the uninitiated, it is an enigma.
This is Theyyam (തെയ്യം).
The deity listens to the prayers, the sorrows, the quiet hopes of his devotees.
I stood in line with a twenty-rupee note, waiting for my chance. In front of me, both young and old—carrying different prayers and hopes—poured their hearts out to the deity. There he sat, covered in red, adorned with silver, a sword resting on his lap.

He shook his leg, his anklets ringing. He held my hand and spoke his blessing. Then, out of the ordinary, he looked me in the eyes and asked:
*"Is this really enough? Shouldn't you be achieving greater things?"*
This happened during a time when I felt no meaning in the work I did This was at a time when I was quietly thinking of building something of my own. But how did the deity know? I had never told him a word.

Then I remembered what I had heard growing up: the deity watches the flame of the lamp before him, and reads the truth within it.

Starting Salvia was the best decision i ever made. Maybe i am just connecting all the dots, I read somewhere that people often find meaning in trivial things, but here I am, a year into this, writing a blog. 

Here, I hope to share memories i do not want to forget.

I also hope that some mysteries stay unanswered
